# How do tasks work?
Decided in #10 and #59

**Applies when:** capturing, completing, repeating or showing tasks.

**Answer:**
- **Capture:** quick capture always creates a task, in the capturer's private Inbox. Later it's sorted into a space and given a date, or turned into another kind of item (a note or an event), keeping what they share: title, description and page. Tasks don't need a date.
- **Ticking:** a ticked task stays on the page, shown as done.
- **Repeating:** a repeating task is one task with a repeat rule, as in Todoist. Ticking it marks that occurrence as done and moves the task to its next date. Each completion is kept as its own record (the task, the date it was done and who ticked it), and the sticker on that day shows that occurrence as done.

**Why:** Capture has to be as fast as Todoist's, so it can't ask what kind of item something is; sorting later, as in Getting Things Done, keeps it instant. Seeing finished work stay on the page is satisfying, and visible progress is part of designing Foyer like a game whose goal is an organised household. Repeating chores are table stakes for replacing Todoist: one task that moves forward keeps lists short, and completion records keep the history of what got done.

**Rejected:** Capturing plain text and deciding its type later, which adds a decision to every item. Ticked tasks disappearing. A separate task for every occurrence of a repeating task, which fills lists with copies.
