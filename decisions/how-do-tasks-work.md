# How do tasks work?
Decided in #10, #21, #22, #23, #59, #27, #28 and #157

**Applies when:** capturing, completing, repeating or showing tasks.

**Answer:**
- **Capture:** quick capture always creates a task, in the capturer's private Inbox. Later it's sorted into a space and given a date, or turned into another kind of item (a note or an event), keeping what they share: title, description and page. Tasks don't need a date.
  - **In the app,** a Capture button on the frame's bar, beside the Calendar, the Notebook and the Tasks, opens one field for the title. Return adds the task to the member's Inbox, says so and clears the field for the next. Nothing asks for a space, a date or a kind. It's the same change as adding a task to the Inbox: `ItemCreate` with its sticker in the Inbox's next free spot, pushed through sync, so it waits in sync's queue while offline.
  - **From an iOS Shortcut,** launched from a widget or the Action Button, the Shortcut sends the title straight to the household with a capture key: `foyer.capture.v1.CaptureService.Capture {title}`, a plain HTTP POST of JSON with `Authorization: Bearer <key>`, never a session. The household makes the task for the key's member as if their device had pushed it: an `ItemCreate` with an id it makes, the household's time as `at`, and the sticker in the next free spot of their Inbox's newest page, or on a new page when it's full. Every device of theirs receives it through sync. Placing stickers lives in `@foyer/shared`, so the household and the apps follow the same rule.
  - A capture with a missing, wrong, replaced or removed key, or from a member whose email has left the allowlist, is refused as unauthenticated. A title that's blank or too long is refused as an invalid argument, with a sentence the Shortcut shows. A key makes at most 100 captures a day, counted by UTC as the live lane's limit is (`what-does-the-live-lane-send.md`); the household refuses the rest as resource exhausted, with "This key has made 100 captures today. Replace it if you didn't make them." A refused capture doesn't count. The Shortcuts app can't queue, so a capture made offline isn't saved, and sending the same capture twice makes two tasks.
- **Capture keys:** each member has at most one, stored in the household's `capture_keys` table: `id`, a UUID version 7; `member_id`; `token_hash`, a SHA-256 hash of the key, which is 32 random bytes, as sessions keep theirs; `created_at`; `last_used_at`, updated at most once a day; and `captures_day`, the UTC day of its last capture, with `captures`, how many it made that day, stored so that the limit holds while the household sleeps. A replaced key starts again at zero, since it has a row of its own. The key is shown once, when it's made, and never sent anywhere after that. It works only for `Capture`, and doesn't expire. It stops working when it's replaced or removed, or while its member's email is off the allowlist.
  - Calls in `CaptureService`, for a signed-in member: `MakeKey {}` returns `{key}`, replacing any key they had; `RemoveKey {}` removes it; `GetKey {}` returns `{exists, created_at, last_used_at}`, which the account menu's Shortcuts entry shows.
- **Title, date and time:** a task's title can't be blank, and is at most 500 characters. A date is a day with no time. A task with a date can also have a time of day, with no duration, which puts it at a point on its day page's timeline (`what-does-a-stickers-position-mean.md`) and which reminders (#35) can use later. Moving it to another date, or ticking it to its next one, keeps its time.
- **Space pages:** each space's main view is its page, the Inbox included, with its tasks as stickers. Each new task's sticker goes in the next free spot below the last one, and keeps its spot. When the next sticker doesn't fit, it goes on the space's next page. Tasks on a full page stay where they are, ticked and open, with the scribbles next to them, like a filled notebook. A space opens on its newest page, with a toggle to its list.
- **A space's list follows its pages:** tasks are in sticker order, page by page, top to bottom, then left to right. Moving a sticker on the page reorders the list. Nothing extra is stored.
- **Today** lists the tasks dated today or earlier, overdue first, then in their space's page order. "Today" is the device's own date. It shows only open tasks.
- **Ticking:** every tick adds a completion record, whether or not the task repeats. A task is done when it has a completion for its current occurrence: a repeating task's date, and none for a task that doesn't repeat. So a task that stops repeating is open, whatever occurrences were ticked before.
  - A completion is stored in the household's `completions` table: `id`, a UUID version 7 made by the device that ticks; `item_id`; `occurrence`, the date of the occurrence it completes, empty for a task that doesn't repeat; `done_at`, the tick's time by the device's clock; and `done_by`.
  - `TaskTick {completion_id, item_id, occurrence, next_date}` adds a completion. If the occurrence already has one, nothing changes. `TaskUntick {item_id, occurrence}` removes it, leaving a tombstone.
  - A tick and an untick of the same occurrence settle by when they were made: the one made last wins, by the device's clock.
  - Anyone who can see a task can tick or untick it, whoever ticked it.
  - Devices receive completions as `Completion {id, item_id, occurrence, done_at, done_by}`. They belong to their task's space, so they reach the same members, move with the task and are deleted with it.
  - A ticked task stays on the page, shown as done. Lists hide ticked tasks, with a "Show done" toggle that each device keeps for itself, unsynced. Today shows only open tasks.
- **Repeating:** a repeating task is one task with a repeat rule, as in Todoist. Ticking it marks that occurrence as done and moves the task to its next date. Each completion is kept as its own record (the task, the date it was done and who ticked it), and the sticker on that day shows that occurrence as done.
  - **The rule** is an iCalendar RRULE, as Google Calendar stores repeating events, such as `FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=TU`. Foyer takes the part its picker offers: daily; weekly on chosen days; monthly on a day of the month, counted from its end when negative, or on a weekday's place in the month, such as `BYDAY=-1FR` for the last Friday; and yearly on a day and month; each every N, up to 999. The household refuses any other part, and keeps a rule as `formatRepeat` in `@foyer/shared` writes it. A part a rule leaves out comes from the task's date, as in iCalendar. A month or year without the rule's day is skipped, as in iCalendar.
  - **Where the next date comes from** is part of each rule, as Todoist's "every" and "every!" are. On its `schedule`, the next date is the rule's first date after the occurrence ticked and after today, counting the interval from the occurrence, so bins every Tuesday stay on Tuesdays. From `completion`, the rule counts from the day it was ticked, so the plants are watered 3 days after the last watering; such a rule counts only days, weeks, months or years.
  - **Ticking an overdue task skips what was missed:** a weekly task due two Tuesdays ago and ticked today moves to the next Tuesday after today. Only today's tick is recorded. "Today" is the device's own date, so the device works out the next date and sends it.
  - **Fields:** items gain `repeat`, the rule, empty for a task that doesn't repeat, and `repeat_from`, `schedule` or `completion`. A repeating task always has a date: its next occurrence. The household refuses clearing it, and refuses a rule on a task without one, so a device setting a rule on a task without a date sends the rule's first date from today in the same push.
  - **Changes:** `ItemSetRepeat {item_id, repeat, repeat_from}` sets the rule, or stops the task repeating when empty; the rule and where its next date comes from change together, and the change made last wins. On a repeating task, `TaskTick` names its occurrence, the task's date, and its `next_date`. The household records the completion, and sets the task's date to `next_date` with the tick's time, so it settles with other date changes: the one made last wins. `TaskUntick` removes that completion, and puts the date back to the occurrence if the tick moved it and nothing has changed it since.
  - **Stickers:** a repeating task has one sticker on its space's page, which shows its next date, and one on each calendar page on its next date. Ticking it leaves its calendar stickers on the day ticked, shown as done for that occurrence, and places new ones on its next date; unticking removes those (`what-does-a-stickers-position-mean.md`).

**Why:** Capture has to be as fast as Todoist's, so it can't ask what kind of item something is; sorting later, as in Getting Things Done, keeps it instant. The native app is iPad-only, and the Action Button is on the iPhone, so a Shortcut that calls the household works there without Foyer open and without native code. A Shortcut may go unused for months, so its key doesn't expire, and since it can only add tasks to its member's Inbox, one key per member is enough. A key that leaks, from a shared Shortcut or a screenshot, could fill its member's Inbox with thousands of tasks before anyone notices, and every capture counts against the free plan's daily requests; 100 a day is far more than anyone captures by hand. Seeing finished work stay on the page is satisfying, and visible progress is part of designing Foyer like a game whose goal is an organised household. Repeating chores are table stakes for replacing Todoist: one task that moves forward keeps lists short, and completion records keep the history of what got done. RRULEs are the one way of describing repeats that Google Calendar also uses, so Foyer doesn't keep a second one next to it. Some chores follow the calendar and others follow when they were last done, so each rule says which. Ticking a chore that was missed for weeks should leave it ready for its next day, not for the ones already gone. One way of ticking every task, repeating or not, keeps that history for all of them. Anyone can untick a task, as anyone can edit what's in a space they see (`who-can-see-what.md`). Lists hide ticked tasks, as Todoist's do, so they show what's left to do, while the page keeps the record.

**Rejected:** Captures without a limit, since replacing a key stops a leak, but only once its member notices. A duration on tasks, which would show them as blocks like events, and no times on tasks. Capturing plain text and deciding its type later, which adds a decision to every item. A Shortcut that opens Foyer at its capture field, which opens Safari or the app for every capture. App Intents and a widget built into the iPad app, which need native code and new dependencies, and don't help the iPhone. Ticked tasks disappearing from the page. `done_at` and `done_by` fields on the task, which would leave two ways of ticking once tasks repeat. Only the person who ticked a task unticking it. Keeping ticked tasks in lists, crossed out, or moving them to a "Done" group at the end. A separate task for every occurrence of a repeating task, which fills lists with copies. Foyer's own fields for repeat rules, a second way of describing repeats next to Google's. Every rule following the schedule, or every rule counting from the tick. Moving an overdue task one occurrence at a time, so missed ones are ticked one by one. Carrying open tasks over to a space's new page, which leaves the ink written next to them behind; and clearing ticked tasks off a full page. A list order stored apart from the page, and sorting a space's list by date.
