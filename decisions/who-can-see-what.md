# Who can see what?
Decided in #10

**Applies when:** creating an item, deciding which devices receive a change, or designing anything about sharing or privacy.

**Answer:** Spaces decide. Every task, event and note lives in exactly one space, and the space decides who can see and change what's in it. Each person has a personal space, and shared spaces hold what several people do together; every member of a space can edit its items. There are no per-item rules. Quick captures sit in the capturer's private Inbox until they're filed into a space.

Ink on calendar pages is the one exception: it's always shared with the whole household, even though the stickers under it keep their own space's visibility. Private handwriting belongs in a personal notebook or an item's body page.

**Why:** One rule per container is easier to understand and to enforce than permissions on every item. The calendar is the household's shared board, like one on the fridge, and keeping its ink in one shared layer avoids the scope of separate boards or layers.

**Rejected:** Per-item visibility, which may be useful later but adds complexity now. A separate calendar board per person or space, which means hopping between boards. Stacked transparent ink layers per space, which is more scope.
