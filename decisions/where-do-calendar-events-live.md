# Where do calendar events live?
Decided in #25, #60, #13, #26, #165, #163 and #72

**Applies when:** showing, creating, moving or storing events, reading repeating events, keeping them in step with Google, archiving or restoring a space, or asking Google for access.

**Answer:** In Google Calendar, which stays the source of truth. Every event in Foyer is a Google Calendar event: creating an event in Foyer, or turning a task into one, creates it in Google, and every change is written back. An event's body page and its sticker position live in Foyer, linked to the Google event.

Foyer works only with calendars it creates for itself, one Foyer calendar per space except the Inbox, and has read and write access to those and nothing else (Google's `calendar.app.created` permission):
- each person connects their own Google account: the one they sign in with. Foyer refuses any other account they pick in Google's screen;
- a personal space's calendar lives in its owner's account, seen only by them;
- a shared space's calendar lives in its creator's account if they've connected Google, and otherwise in the account of the member who connected first. If nobody has connected yet, the next member who connects holds every shared space without a calendar. Its holder never changes once the calendar exists, because that permission can't move a calendar to another account. Its holder shares it with the household's members in Google Calendar's own settings, which that permission can't change, and Foyer reminds them to;
- a member's Inbox has no calendar, because quick capture always makes a task;
- each calendar is named after its space, as "Foyer · Groceries", is renamed with it, and takes the time zone of the device its holder connected from. Spaces made before someone connects get their calendar when they do.

What members see, sent through sync and set only by the household:
- every member who sees a space sees who holds its calendar (`Space.calendar_member_id`) and its state (`calendar_state`): `none` until it exists, `syncing`, or `paused` when its holder's access stopped working or they disconnected;
- each member alone receives their own connection (`GoogleConnection`): `connected`, or `needs_reconnecting` once Google stops accepting the access. A member who never connected, or who disconnected, has none. Connecting again resumes their paused calendars; disconnecting revokes Foyer's access at Google and leaves the calendars and their events there.

Google's tokens and ids never leave the server, and backups and exports (#56) leave them out. The household keeps them in `google_connections` (one row per member: the refresh token, the permissions granted, the state, the time zone, when they connected and the current access token), `space_calendars` (one row per space: its holder, Google's id for the calendar, the name and time zone Google has, the sync token, and when it was last read or found deleted) `google_events` (one row per event in Google, an occurrence of a series included: its calendar's and its own Google id, its etag and the fields Google last had) and `google_series` (one row per repeating series: its calendar's and its own Google id, the `series_id` its occurrences share, and how far ahead its occurrences have been made). Connecting is a call (`foyer.calendar.v1.CalendarService`), not a change through sync, as it happens outside the household's data.

Events in people's other Google calendars don't appear in Foyer; anything the household should see goes in a Foyer calendar.

What Foyer keeps of an event is its title, description and when it happens (`what-is-an-item.md`). Everything else, such as its location, guests, reminders and colour, stays in Google untouched: Foyer's writes change only the fields it keeps, so adding one later needs no migration, as reading Google again fills it in. A title Google leaves blank is "(No title)", as Google Calendar shows it. A description is plain text in Foyer, so one written as HTML in Google, as Google Calendar's own editor saves it, is read as plain text: its line breaks, paragraphs and list items on lines of their own, each link's address after its text when the two differ, other tags dropped and entities decoded. A description with no tags is kept as it is.

Keeping events in step with Google, done by the household with each calendar's holder's access:
- **Reading Google:** the household reads each calendar's changes with Google's sync tokens as soon as a device connects, every minute while any device is connected, and every 15 minutes otherwise. If Google drops a sync token, it reads the whole calendar again, and events no longer in it are deleted in Foyer.
- **From Google to Foyer:** an event new in Google becomes an event in Foyer, with an id the household makes, no creator (`created_by` empty, which the app shows as added in Google Calendar) and Google's time as `created_at`. An event deleted in Google is deleted in Foyer, with its stickers. An event moved in Google from one of Foyer's calendars to another is moved in Foyer to that calendar's space, as moving it in Foyer does, keeping its id, its stickers, its body page and the items under it, unless a move made in Foyer later wins. Google reports the move as a deletion in one calendar and a new event in the other, in either order, so the household asks Google whether an event that left one of Foyer's calendars is in another of them before deleting it.
- **From Foyer to Google:** each committed change to an event is written to Google afterwards, with the event's whole current version of the fields Foyer keeps, and tried again, waiting longer each time, until it works. The one exception is a description unchanged in Foyer since it was read from Google, which is left as Google has it, so renaming an event in Foyer keeps the formatting its description has in Google. Deleting an event in Foyer deletes it in Google. Moving it to another space moves it to that space's calendar: with Google's move within one account, or made in the new calendar and deleted from the old across accounts. Its Google id changes; its Foyer id doesn't.
- **Conflicts:** the change made last wins, field by field, as everywhere in Foyer (`how-do-devices-stay-in-sync.md`). Google gives only one time for a whole event, its `updated`, so a change read from Google sets each field that changed in Google with that time, and loses to a change made in Foyer later. Writes to Google name the version they change; if the event changed there since, the household reads it, settles it this way, and writes again.
- **Refused for good:** if Google refuses a change as invalid, rather than failing, the household takes Google's version back into Foyer as a change made then, so the two match again. An event Google refuses to create is deleted in Foyer.
- **Paused spaces:** changes to a paused space's events save in Foyer, and are written to Google once its holder reconnects. If the same event changed in Google meanwhile, the two settle as any conflict does.
- **Calendars deleted in Google:** Foyer makes the calendar again in the same account, and writes the space's events back into it.
- **Archived spaces** (`who-can-see-what.md`): the household stops reading and writing an archived space's calendar, which stays in Google with its events. It doesn't make, rename or make again its calendar, or make its series' occurrences, and writes waiting for it wait. Restoring reads it again and settles any changes made in Google meanwhile, as for a paused space. An event Google reports in another of Foyer's calendars, which Foyer knew in an archived space's, is taken as moved there without asking Google, as for a paused space's; one moved into an archived space's calendar is followed there.
- A shared space whose holder leaves the household while it's archived is taken over once it's restored, which is built by #155.
- **Calls to Google:** one run of the household makes at most 40 calls to Google, refreshing access included, so that it stays within the free plan's 50 calls to other hosts. Calendars to make or rename go first, then the writes waiting longest, then the calendars due to be read, those read longest ago first, then series' occurrences. What's left waits for the next run, which starts at once.

The household keeps beside each event its Google calendar and event ids, Google's version tag (etag) and the fields as Google last had them, and for each space's calendar Google's sync token, its time zone and when it was last read. None of it reaches devices.

Repeating events stay Google's: every occurrence is an event of its own in Foyer, with its own stickers on its own day, and the whole series is changed in Google Calendar. Making a repeating event, or changing a whole series, stays in Google Calendar.
- **Occurrences:** the household makes an event for each occurrence Google lists for a series (its instances), from the start of last month to 18 months ahead, both at midnight UTC, and makes the next ones as that window moves on a day at a time. No device, and nothing in the household, works out repeat rules. Each occurrence has its series' `series_id` (`what-is-an-item.md`), and a row in `google_events` under its own Google id. Occurrences that have passed are kept, like any past event, and a series Foyer reads for the first time gets occurrences from the start of last month, not from when it began.
- **One occurrence, in Foyer:** changing its title, description or time, or deleting it, changes only that occurrence, written to Google as a change to that instance, as Google Calendar's "This event" does. Moving an occurrence to another space, or an item with one under it, is refused, since Google moves only whole series: "A repeating event moves with its series, in Google Calendar."
- **The series or one occurrence, in Google** (its rule, time, title or end, deleting it, or moving the series to another of Foyer's calendars): read back like any change. Occurrences are added, changed, moved or deleted to match, keeping their ids, stickers and body pages where they still exist. Google gives every occurrence a new id when the series' time changes, so an occurrence Google no longer lists becomes the one it lists for the same series on the same day, keeping its id. A deleted occurrence's items are moved up as for any deleted event.
- **Calls to Google:** a series' occurrences are listed again whenever the series or one of its occurrences changes in Google, and once a day for the next day's, at a call or two each, within each run's calls to Google (see Keeping events in step with Google).
- **A calendar deleted in Google** takes its series' occurrences with it in Foyer: Foyer doesn't make series, so it can't write them back.

**Why:** Google Calendar is already complete, and people keep using it outside Foyer; letting Foyer have events of its own, or edit whole series, would slowly rebuild it. Making each occurrence an event of its own lets everything built for events work for occurrences unchanged, and leaves repeat rules to Google, so no device has to work them out. Keeping only the fields Foyer shows leaves the rest to Google, where people edit them. Reading HTML as plain text keeps a description the same kind of text everywhere in Foyer, and leaving a description Foyer didn't change as Google has it keeps formatting people added in Google Calendar. Settling field by field, as Foyer does everywhere, keeps an edit made in Foyer and one made in Google to another field. Saving changes to a paused space and making a deleted calendar again keep what people did in Foyer, rather than losing it or stopping the space until someone acts. One calendar per space keeps the link clear and makes Google's sharing match Foyer's spaces, so a personal event stays private in Google too. Access limited to Foyer's own calendars keeps everything else private from Foyer. Using the account people sign in with stores nothing new about them. Showing every member a space's holder and state tells them why a shared space stopped updating and whose access to fix. This relies on Google letting an app see only the calendars it created; if that permission doesn't work as documented (#25), revisit this record.

**Rejected:** A series as one item, with its occurrences worked out on each device, which would have every device expand repeat rules identically in each calendar's time zone, and compute calendar stickers that are stored rows today. Keeping Google's HTML and showing descriptions formatted, which would change what a description is in Foyer. Refusing changes to a paused space's events. When a calendar is deleted in Google, deleting the space's events in Foyer too, or pausing the space until its holder makes it again. Connecting any Google account the person chooses, which would store a second account's id and email for each member. Waiting for a shared space's creator to connect, so that a space made by someone who never connects could never hold events. Showing each member only their own connection, so a shared space whose holder's access broke stops updating without saying why. Events that exist only in Foyer, which grow into a second calendar to keep in step with Google. A single Foyer calendar for the whole household, which would show every personal event to everyone in Google Calendar. Access to all of a person's calendars, which Foyer doesn't need. One shared "hub" Google account, which personal calendars don't fit.
